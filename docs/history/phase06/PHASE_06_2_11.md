# Phase 06.2.11 — Emergency digestion and starvation diagnostics

## Aim
Test whether carnivores die with food still in their stomach. This is a targeted, resource-conserving test rather than extinction prevention. The baseline `unified-control` reproduces the 06.2.10 unified configuration with the new feature disabled. The two interventions speed up digestion only when the reserve energy is below a threshold.

## Conditions (10 seeds × 50,000 ticks each)
- `unified-control`: no adaptive digestion (06.2.10 unified baseline)
- `adaptive-4x`: when reserve E < 2, digest stomach E up to 4× normal rate
- `adaptive-8x`: when reserve E < 4, digest stomach E up to 8× normal rate

Only transfers between existing stomach and reserve accounts change. No new N/E is created; the digestion multiplier affects energy transfer, not nutrients. The stomach fullness and feeding hysteresis remain unchanged. All options default OFF for backward compatibility.

## Commands (PowerShell, project root)
```powershell
cargo test --workspace
powershell.exe -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\run_phase06211.ps1 -Ticks 1000 -SeedCount 2
powershell.exe -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\run_phase06211.ps1
```

Outputs: `results/phase06211/<condition>/seed-<seed>.csv` and `summary.csv`.

## New cumulative CSV fields
- `emergency_digestion_ticks`: predator-ticks in emergency digestion mode
- `emergency_digestion_energy`: total energy transferred during emergency digestion
- `predator_death_stomach_energy`: stomach energy at predator death, summed
- `predator_death_stomach_nutrients`: stomach nutrients at predator death, summed
- `predator_deaths_with_stomach_energy`: deaths with positive stomach E
- `predator_low_reserve_with_stomach_ticks`: predator-ticks with reserve E below threshold and stomach E remaining
- `predator_starvation_with_stomach_energy`: starvation deaths despite stomach E remaining

## Interpretation
If emergency digestion substantially lowers `predator_starvation_with_stomach_energy` without reducing herbivore survival, investigate physiology further. If it does not, examine prey availability, energy costs, and population overshoot instead. This is not a guaranteed coexistence fix.
